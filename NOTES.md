## How it works
- app.py fetches data from the FPL Draft site (team → league lookup, league details, who owns whom) and the classic FPL site (fixtures + difficulty), then rates every player 0–100.
- Three API routes: /api/team/<team id> (what the browser uses), /api/team/<team id>/trade (the trade analyzer) and /api/league/<league id> (used by check_league.py and tests). They all build the page data with load_league(); the two team routes find the league with league_for_team().
- All requests to the Draft site go through fetch(), which turns failures into an FplError with a friendly message. The routes just catch FplError.
- app.py also works out the waiver suggestions (waiver_targets) and each squad's best eleven and strength (best_eleven, squad_strength), so that logic is covered by tests.
- static/index.html is the page you see in the browser. It reads ?team= from the address, loads that team, and puts the personal link back in the address bar. It draws what the server worked out.
- check_league.py loads a league through the app and prints a health report. Run: python check_league.py <league id> or python check_league.py --team <team id>. Lines with !! need a look.
- tests/test_app.py uses fake data, so tests never call the real servers.
- Run locally: python app.py, then open http://127.0.0.1:5000
- On Render: build command `pip install -r requirements.txt`, start command `gunicorn app:app --workers 1 --threads 4 --timeout 60`. Render picks the Python version from .python-version (3.12). Deploy steps are in README.md.

## How the rating works
- Five stats per player: form, points per game, xGI per 90 (in proportion to minutes/180 if under 180 minutes), minutes share, fixture ease over the next 3 GWs (sum of 6 - difficulty; doubles count twice, blanks count zero).
- Each stat is divided by its 95th percentile (SCALE_PERCENTILE) among players with 180+ minutes (MIN_MINUTES) and capped at 1.0. Early in the season, before anyone has 180 minutes, the scale uses everyone who has played.
- Then the stats are combined with position weights (WEIGHTS), times 100, times availability (chance of playing, or 0 if injured/suspended).
- score_players also returns a breakdown: rating points per piece (form, ppg, xgi, mins, fix = 100 x weight x stat) plus avail (what an injury doubt takes off, 0 or less). The pieces add up to the rating, give or take rounding. Every player on the page carries it.
- The stats are gathered by raw_stats() and the yardstick (what each stat is divided by) is rating_scale(). score_players works the yardstick out from the players it is given unless one is passed in as `scale`; only Back in play passes one in, to rate a made-up "when fit" line with the real players' yardstick (see "How Back in play works"). A test checks the ratings are identical either way.

## How the season view works
- VIEWS in app.py holds each view's settings: weights (WEIGHTS / SEASON_WEIGHTS), the injury rule and whether return dates are used. The window length comes from view_windows(): "week" = LOOKAHEAD (5); "season" = break_window().
- break_window(deadlines, next_gw): the first gameweek g from next_gw where the deadline of g+1 is more than BREAK_GAP_DAYS (10) after g's means a break after g, so the window is next_gw..g. Kept between BREAK_MIN_WEEKS (3) and BREAK_MAX_WEEKS (10), never past GW38; SEASON_LOOKAHEAD (8) if no break is found. Deadlines come from boot events data (gameweek_deadlines). Checked on the real calendar: from GW6 the window is GW6-10 (15 days between the GW10 and GW11 deadlines).
- The league data also has "windows": {view: {from, to}}, which the page uses for the button labels.
- Weights are dicts per position, {piece: weight}, each adding up to 1 (a test checks this). Missing pieces count as 0.
- New pieces (season only): cs = CS_BASELINE (2.0) minus expected goals conceded per 90, so lower xGC = better; dc = defensive_contribution per 90; crea = creativity per 90. All count in proportion under MIN_MINUTES, like xGI, and use the same 95th-percentile scale.
- Season weights: form drops to 0.15 everywhere (Next 5 has 0.30), and the freed weight goes to fixtures, xGI and clean sheets. GKP adds cs; DEF adds cs, dc and crea (creativity for attacking full backs); MID adds dc; FWD leans more on xGI and fixtures. All first guesses. Fixtures get a bit less weight over the longer window.
- Injuries in the season view: chance >= SEASON_FULL_FROM (75) counts as fully fit; 50% and below are unchanged (50% halves the rating). A player at 0% who has a news_return date counts as available for the share of the window's gameweeks whose deadline is on or after that date (availability() with window_deadlines); no date, or a doubt above 0%, works as before. Waiver suggestions still use the real next-week chance, so an injured player isn't suggested. The player's "chance" field (used for the "!" warning and waiver rules) is always the real next-week chance.
- load_league(league_id, view) rates everyone in both views: week_score and season_score on every player; score, breakdown and fixtures follow the chosen view. Unknown views fall back to "week". All three routes accept ?view=season.
- The page: toggle in the league bar reloads with ?view=, the link keeps &view=season, the free agents table shows both ratings (chosen one in bold, sorted by it), fixture strips show the view's window, trades send the view.

## How squad strength history works
- strength_snapshot(by_view, today) in app.py turns load_league's data for both views into one day's record: season, date, current_gw, next_gw, windows, "managers" (entry_id, team, manager and, per view, strength, formation, pieces, xi = [[player id, rating], ...]) and "players" (id as text -> name, for the ids in any best eleven, so the file reads on its own).
- save_strength(league_id, snapshot) adds it to history/<season>/strength_<league>.json: `{"league_id", "season", "snapshots": [one entry per line, oldest first]}`. One entry per date: the same numbers as the latest entry (ignoring the date) add nothing, so a quiet day makes no commit and no redeploy; a second run on the same date with different numbers replaces that date's entry. A file it can't read raises instead of being written over. The season comes from the date (season_label), so a new season starts a new file.
- save_snapshot.py calls it after saving the results, and a failure to load the strengths is only printed ("!! Couldn't save the squad strengths"), so the results, which can't be rebuilt, are still committed by the workflow. The workflow itself is unchanged: it already runs the script and `git add history`.
- Size: about 10 KB a day for 12 managers (both views), so roughly 2 MB over a season if every day changed; days between match days mostly won't.
- Reading it back (built 2026-10-19): `saved_strengths(league_id)` reads this season's file ([] if it's missing or unreadable, so a bad file only means no history on the page; `save_strength` is the one that refuses to write over it). `strength_baseline(snapshots, current_gw)` picks the last snapshot from before the current gameweek (so "since the end of GW6" while GW7 is on, and None until a snapshot from an earlier gameweek exists). `strength_moves(data, baseline)` compares the live numbers (load_league's data for the chosen view) with it. `strength_chart(data, snapshots)` gives every saved day (date and gameweek) and each manager's strength on it in the chosen view, None until two days are saved. `strength_history()` bundles them as {since, moves, chart}. The `/api/league/<id>/squads` route adds "movement" to each manager, plus "since" ({gw, date}) and "chart" at the top; `league_squads()` itself is unchanged. No new requests: a local file read on top of load_league.
- The split (strength_moves): strength is the average rating of the eleven, so one player's rating change is 1/11 of it. `same_players` = the rating changes of players who are in both elevens, added up and divided by the size of the eleven; `new_players` = the change minus that, which is (ratings of the players who came in minus the ratings of those who left, the leavers at their old rating) divided by the size. `change` is the difference of the two strengths the page shows, and both parts are rounded so they add up to it (never -0.0, see `tidy`). "Different players in the eleven" covers a claim or a trade, but also an injury or a bench player forcing his way in. `pieces` is the change in each piece of xi_pieces for the whole eleven: the file keeps each player's rating but not his pieces, so the pieces can't be split between players who stayed and players who came in. Riser and faller are the biggest rating moves among the players who stayed, only if at least MOVER_MIN_RATING (1). A manager is left out (movement is None) if they weren't in the baseline, have no eleven, or have a different number of players in it (the maths needs both elevens the same size).
- The page (league.html): renderStrengthChart() draws the line chart in plain SVG with the league race's styles (x is the date, so a missing day leaves a gap; the gameweek labels sit under their days with a dashed line between gameweeks; labels at the end for the strongest team and you), movedBlock() draws the block in an opened row, and trend() draws the green or red change (always with its sign). The chart is hidden when it is None.

## How the league race works
- league_history(details) in app.py, returned as "history" in the league data. Built from details["matches"] (already fetched), so no extra requests.
- Only matches with finished = true count; future gameweeks are listed with 0 points.
- Matches (and standings) name managers by league entry ID; league_entries maps it ("id" -> "entry_id") to the team IDs the rest of the app uses.
- Each week: W = 3 league points (WIN_POINTS), D = 1 (DRAW_POINTS), L = 0. A match with no opponent score gets no result.
- Per manager: points, results, league_points (running total), behind (vs the leader that week), scored, table_total (the official table's total). Sorted by league points, then points scored. Plus the league's average score each week.
- None for classic-scoring leagues (no matches) and before any gameweek finishes; the page then shows a short message.
- The charts page draws it with plain SVG (no chart library). Lines are nudged a pixel or two apart by table position so tied managers stay visible (visual only). The grid scrolls sideways once there are many gameweeks, newest weeks shown first, team names stay put.
- check_league.py checks everyone has weekly results and that our league points equal the official table.

## How suggestions work
- Per position: my players weakest first vs free agents best first, paired one-for-one, max 2 pairs per position (PAIRS_PER_POSITION).
- Free agents count if they're at least 75% likely to play (MIN_CHANCE_FOR_WAIVERS). Their rating is already scaled down for the doubt, so a 75% player has to be clearly better to show up.
- A doubtful claim gets a "!" warning and a backup: the best fully fit free agent in the same position that isn't already one of the suggestions.
- A pair only shows if the free agent rates at least 3 points higher (MIN_GAIN).
- Top 5 pairs by gain are shown (MAX_TARGETS).
- "Worth doing" (mark_worth_doing in app.py, "worth" on each swap, and "waiver_verdict" on the team route's data): a swap's lift is xi_after minus xi_before (xi_lift, the two numbers the page shows, so rounded to 0.1). Swaps with a lift of at least WORTH_XI_GAIN (0.5, the same bar trades use) are worth doing, biggest lift first (then biggest gain), and only the first MAX_WORTH_DOING (2) are marked. waiver_targets then lists the worth-doing swaps first and the rest after, each group by gain. waiver_verdict() is "move" if any swap is worth doing, otherwise "none" (also when there are no swaps at all). A swap that only changes the bench lifts nothing, so it's never worth doing. The trade page (?swaps=0) gets neither. The page only draws the tag and the box
- Keep: ?keep=12,34 (set by the Keep button) removes those players from the drop candidates, so the next weakest is paired instead. Only my own players count. They still count in squad strength.
- Each swap also carries: small_sample (drop/claim with under SMALL_SAMPLE_MINUTES = 300), drop_in_xi plus xi_before/xi_after (best-eleven strength with and without the swap, from strength_after_swap), and the route adds "recent": minutes in the last RECENT_GAMEWEEKS (4) gameweeks for every player in a swap, from /element-summary/<id> (one request each, 8 at a time, cached 10 minutes; a failed request just leaves that player out). A real league load went from about 1 to 5 seconds uncached.
- Each pair has a "why" (swap_reasons): claim breakdown minus drop breakdown, piece by piece. Up to 3 reasons (MAX_REASONS) of at least 1 point (MIN_REASON), biggest first; if none are that big, the single biggest. The drop's injury doubt counts as a reason ("Porro is 50% to play"); the claim's own doubt isn't repeated because it has the "!" row. "against" = the biggest piece in the drop's favour ("But Porro has more attacking threat -6").
- Reasons to hold (keep_notes in app.py, returned as "keep_notes": {drop player id: {kind, text}}, drawn under the Fitness line): only for the players the swaps would drop, and it never changes which swaps are suggested (the Keep button is still how you act on it). past_seasons() gets each drop's earlier seasons from the classic site's /element-summary/<id>/ (history_past: points and minutes per season, nothing week by week), matching players by `code` (every player now carries it; Draft and classic ids differ, e.g. Barcola is 628 and 627). The classic /bootstrap-static/ it needs is already fetched for the fixtures, so the only new requests are one per drop (up to 5, 8 at a time, cached 10 minutes); a failed request just means no note. ?swaps=0 (the trade page) skips all of it.
  - "hold" (hold_case): a season counts as proof if it's one of the last HOLD_SEASONS (2) finished seasons (by name, e.g. 2025/26 and 2024/25, from season_label), had at least HOLD_MIN_MINUTES (1500) and at least HOLD_POINTS for his position (GKP 135, DEF 140, MID 150, FWD 130: about the top quarter of the players my league owns, worked out from last season on 2026-10-08). And the drop has to look temporary: he's "out" (chance of playing at most HOLD_DOUBT_MAX (50%), or a milder doubt and 0 minutes in the latest gameweek; a 75% flag on someone playing every week doesn't count), or his points per 90 this season (once he has MIN_MINUTES) is under HOLD_SLUMP_SHARE (70%) of his proven rate. A player who has left his club (status "u", "Has joined ... on loan") gets no note. The text quotes his best qualifying season, his rate per 90 and, for a slump, this season's rate
    - How long he's out (absence_note, gameweeks_out): the gameweeks from the next one whose deadline falls before his return date (read from the news by return_date; deadlines from season_deadlines(), the Draft events, cached), plus how many gameweeks are left after he's back. HOLD_SHORT_GWS (4) or fewer = kind "hold" (Worth holding), HOLD_LONG_GWS (10) or more = "let_go" (Fine to let go; Keep is still offered), in between = "call" (Your call). Back before the next deadline = hold. A doubt with no return date = hold ("only a doubt, so this may not last"). Out with no return date (about half of injuries) = call, saying so. A return date but no deadlines = call. A slump with no injury = hold. The note also carries gws_out (gameweeks missed, or None)
  - "new" (new_signing): no minutes in any past season on record, and his minutes over the latest RECENT_GAMEWEEKS are rising (the later half adds up to more than the earlier half and he played in the latest one but under NEW_SETTLED_MINUTES (80): past that he has settled in, so Jacquet (76, 90, 90, 90) gets nothing). Says it's too early to judge. A fact, not a prediction
- League squads = average rating of each team's best legal eleven: take the minimum at each position (1 GKP, 3 DEF, 2 MID, 1 FWD), then fill the last 4 places with the best players left without breaking a maximum (1 GKP, 5 DEF, 5 MID, 3 FWD). My squad is opened and highlighted, with formation and bench.

## How the weekly lineup works
- week_lineups(players, me, next_gw) in app.py, returned as "lineups" by /api/team/<id> (not with ?swaps=0, so the trade page doesn't get it), drawn by renderLineups() in static/index.html. It uses the data load_league already has, so it makes no requests and costs nothing measurable.
- SHAPES = every legal (DEF, MID, FWD) with ten outfielders, worked out from FORMATION (eight: 5-4-1, 5-3-2, 5-2-3, 4-5-1, 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 3-5-2, 3-4-3), biggest defence first so a tie goes the way best_eleven's fill does. The best lineup is best_eleven() itself (so with no blanks it is exactly the eleven League squads shows); the second best is the best of the other shapes: for each, the top 1 keeper, d defenders, m midfielders, f forwards. A shape the squad can't fill (two forwards for 3-4-3) is skipped. None if the squad is under 11 players; "second" is None if no other shape fits.
- "For the week": a player counts as 0, and so lands on the bench, if his club has no game in next_gw (his fixtures come from the chosen view's window, which always starts at next_gw). That only applies when the fixture data has some game in that gameweek for someone: if the fixtures feed failed (upcoming_fixtures returns empty lists) or the week has no games at all, nobody is benched for it, so a feed problem can't empty your team. A player with 2 games has two chips. His rating is still the view's rating, not a prediction for that one match.
- Bench: the spare keeper first, then the rest best first (blank players last). Second lineup: "change" is its strength minus the best's, computed from the two rounded strengths the page shows so the numbers add up (and never -0.0); rows in it carry new=True for the players who came in, and "out" lists who left the best eleven.

It also asks /api/team/<id>/trade-ideas (with the same league and view) for the Trade ideas by club section, so a failure there only shows an error in that section and the analyzer still works. It also reads &give= and &get= (player IDs) from the address, which the main page's Check this trade link carries: once, on the first load, it ticks those players (only those who really belong to me and to the manager in &with=) and runs the trade.
- static/index.html loads /api/team/<id>/trade-ideas (with league, view and keep) at the end of every render(), so a view change or a Keep press refreshes the club list too. It is a separate request, so the waiver cards and tables show first; the section stays hidden until the team has a drafted squad.
- static/index.html loads /api/team/<id>/returning the same way and at the same moment (league, view and keep), for the Back in play section between Waiver targets and Your team this week. Its Build a trade link opens /trade with &with=<the owner> only: the trade page needs a player on both sides before it ticks anything.
- league.html takes ?league=<id> (and optionally &tab= and &full=1), so the link works for anyone in the league. Banter's data (/api/league/<id>/banter) is asked for as soon as the league opens, because the match-up cards above the choices need it, and again whenever Banter is opened (see "How the pinned match-ups work"); /api/league/<id>/live adds the live scores to the cards. Charts calls /api/league/<id>/charts, only when picked and only once per league. Trades calls /api/league/<id>/deals the same way, and opening the Recap asks for it too (fillDealCard), to draw the Deals card above the recap. Opening Trades also asks /api/league/<id>/moves (fillActivity) for the moves chart, Odds calls /api/league/<id>/odds, and Rivalries gets the head-to-head grid with its manager list; each loads once per league (the odds and moves are not refreshed while a tab stays open). The team ID the main page last used (kept in this browser's localStorage) highlights you in the charts and prefills My team. The page never reads a team ID from the address or writes one to it, so a link copied from the address bar can't carry one. &full=1 shows My team and the way back; the view-only share link has neither.

## How the trade analyzer works
- evaluate_trade(players, me, them, give, get) in app.py. Route: /api/team/<me>/trade?with=<them>&give=12,34&get=56&league=<id>.
- Checks first (TradeError, shown as a 400 with a friendly message): not trading with myself, at least one player each side, give are all mine, get are all theirs, same positions on both sides. Repeated IDs are dropped.
- Then it copies the player list with the traded owners swapped (the real list is never changed) and runs squad_strength for both managers before and after.
- Each side reports strength before/after/change, formation before/after, who joins or leaves the best eleven, and by_position: the change in total rating of each position in the best eleven (xi_by_position). Totals, not averages, so "MID -25, FWD +25" reads as one swap cancelling the other. A formation shift shows up here too (e.g. DEF +50 when a defender takes a midfield spot).
- Verdict: my change <= -TRADE_MIN_GAIN (0.5) = worse; under +0.5 = no change; otherwise good_and_fair if their change >= -FAIR_MARGIN (0.5), else good_but_unfair. 0.5 strength is roughly one starter improving by 5-6 rating points.
- The page ticks players, sends the IDs, and draws the verdict and both panels. It sends ?league= so the server uses the same league as the page.

## How trade ideas work
- trade_ideas(players, me, managers) in app.py, route /api/team/<me>/trade-ideas?league=<id>&view=week|season (same league and view rules as the other team routes). It builds on the same league data as the trade page, so it makes no new requests to the Draft site.
- Every other manager's player (never my own, never a free agent) gets best_offer(): each of my players in his position is tried with evaluate_trade (so the numbers and the verdict are exactly the analyzer's), and the one that lifts my best eleven most wins. A tie goes to the offer that costs them least, then to the lower-rated player. If the best lift is under TRADE_MIN_GAIN (0.5) the player has no idea. One-for-one only, so the positions always match.
- The result groups every player by Premier League club (the three-letter code, A-Z): {club, best_gain (the biggest lift among its ideas, or None), players: [{id, name, pos, score, total, owner, owner_name, mine, idea}]}, players in squad order (GKP first) and best first. idea = {give, give_name, me, them, verdict}, where me and them are the change in each best eleven's strength and verdict is good_and_fair or good_but_unfair. The page only draws it: the sort toggle orders the clubs by best_gain, "Check this trade" ticks the two players in the analyzer's lists and runs analyzeTrade().
- Cost: one evaluate_trade per (my player, their player) pair of the same position, about 700 on the real league (0.4 seconds on my computer). Each one copies the player list, so the cost grows with the square of the squad size if bigger trades are ever added.
- Claims for free agents (claim_idea in app.py, "claim" on each free agent's row, "best_claim" on each club): the free agent replaces my weakest player in his position (never one in ?keep=, the same rule a waiver swap uses), and the lift is strength_after_swap minus my strength now, rounded to 0.1, which is the number the waiver cards show as xi_after minus xi_before. Dropping the weakest always leaves the strongest squad, so no other drop needs trying. A claim is None if he doesn't rate above that player, or the lift is under WORTH_XI_GAIN (0.5, the waivers' bar). It is a separate field from "idea" on purpose: "idea" and "best_gain" stay trade-only, so the trade page is unchanged. Rows also carry "chance" and "news", so the page can show a doubt or injury. Free agents are all judged, including the injured (their rating is already scaled down, so they rarely clear the bar). The route takes ?keep= like the team route.
- Cost of claims on the real league (2026-10-16, 667 players, 487 free agents): 0.3 seconds on top of about 1.0 for the trade ideas, 1.3 in all with the league cached (the first call after a cold start was about 9 seconds, nearly all the league load). 19 free agents had a claim in Next 5.

## How Back in play works
- Why it exists: the rating is built from recent form (points over the last 30 days), points per game, per-90 stats and minutes share. A player who has been out has zeros for most of them, and his rating stays low for weeks after he is fit. On 2026-11-10 Saliba (fit, no minutes all season) rated 18.7, all of it his fixtures, 105th of 159 free defenders, and Scott (6.0 points a game before he was out) had a form piece of 0.
- load_returning(data, me, keep) in app.py, route /api/team/<me>/returning?league=<id>&view=week|season&keep=12,34 (same league, view and keep rules as trade-ideas). `data` is load_league's result. It returns {league_id, view, me, next_gw, shown (RETURN_SHOWN), groups}; the page (renderReturning() in index.html) only draws it.
- Step 1, who is coming back: return_status(player, deadlines, next_gw) gives {group, text, gws_out} or None.
  - "back": chance of playing at least MIN_CHANCE_FOR_WAIVERS (75, the same bar a waiver claim uses), form 0 (no points in the last 30 days) and a flag raised this season (news_added on or after 1 July of the season). Text: "Fit again: flagged on 5 Oct, and no points in the last 30 days."
  - "soon": out with a return date that costs RETURN_SOON_GWS (3) gameweeks or fewer (counted by gameweeks_out, the same deadline rule the hold notes use), or a doubt (above 0% but under 75%) with no return date. The text is the feed's news plus what it costs: ", in time for GW11.", ", so he misses 3 gameweeks.", or ". That date has passed and he's still flagged."
  - "out": out for longer than that, or no return date (also a return date when there are no deadlines to count with).
  - None: has left his club (status "u"), isn't due back before GW38, is fit with points lately, or is fit and was never flagged (he is just not being picked).
- Step 2, what to rate him on. With MIN_MINUTES (180) or more this season: this season's numbers. With fewer: his past seasons from the classic site (classic_history, one request each), but only if his pre-season draft rank (the Draft feed's draft_rank) is inside managers x RETURN_RANK_PER_MANAGER (15: 180 in my league, everyone who would have been drafted), best ranked first and at most RETURN_MAX_LOOKUPS (30). proven_season() picks the latest of the last HOLD_SEASONS (2) seasons with RETURN_PROOF_MINUTES (900) or more. No such season, or the request failed: he can't be rated, so he is left off.
- Step 3, the rating when fit. as_if_fit(el, last, games_so_far) makes a copy of his line in the Draft feed: status "a", 100% to play, no news, and form = the larger of his form and his points per game. On a past season his minutes and the four season totals the rating uses (expected goal involvements, expected goals conceded, defensive contribution, creativity) are that season's times (gameweeks played so far / 38), so every per-90 number and his minutes share are last season's, and his points per game are last season's points per start (or per 90 minutes if that gives more games: the feed has no count of appearances). His club, and so his fixtures, are today's. fit_ratings() runs score_players on those lines with the real players' yardstick (rating_scale of the whole real list), so "now" and "when fit" are measured the same way.
- Step 4, who is listed. returning_players() keeps a player if his rating when fit is at least RETURN_MIN_FIT (60) and at least RETURN_MIN_GAP (10) above his rating now, sorts each group best when fit first, and adds: owner and owner_name, mine, basis ("this" or "last"), proof (a sentence: "6.0 points a game this season (446 minutes, 5 starts)." or "137 points last season (2,614 minutes). No minutes yet this season."), small_sample (rated on this season with under SMALL_SAMPLE_MINUTES, 300) and, for a free agent, claim = claim_idea() run on a copy of the players in which he has his rating when fit: {drop, drop_name, drop_score, me}, or None if he still wouldn't lift my best eleven by WORTH_XI_GAIN (0.5). So the claim is the claim Players by club would show if he really rated that.
- The page: three groups drawn like the clubs in Players by club (same styles), the third closed until opened, the first RETURN_SHOWN (5) of a group with "Show all N". Which groups are open and where Show all was pressed are remembered across a view change or a Keep press. It is a separate request made at the end of render(), so the waiver cards show first, and an error only shows inside this section. A team with no squad doesn't get the section.
- Cost on the real league (2026-11-10, 672 players): 101 players had a return status (34 back, 18 soon, 49 out), 71 of them with under 180 minutes, 11 of those ranked in the top 180, so 11 requests to the classic site; 2.0 seconds cold on top of the league load, nothing once cached (10 minutes). No extra request to the Draft site: the player list is read from the cache load_league has just filled.

## How deals work
- load_deals(league_id) in app.py, route /api/league/<id>/deals, drawn by static/league.html (the Deals card above the Recap, and the Trades choice). It reads the Draft site's /draft/league/<id>/trades and /transactions (note the extra /draft/ in these two addresses, which the other league feeds don't have), the live points of the finished gameweeks it needs (/event/<gw>/live, one request each, cached 10 minutes; only from the earliest trade's first gameweek to the latest finished one, so 2 requests today and up to 38 late in the season) and, for the free agents everyone missed, our ratings (load_league). The claims and the ratings are optional; the trades are not. About 4-5 seconds cold on my computer.
- Only trades with state "p" count: it's the only state seen on real data (both trades, players have moved), and anything else is assumed not to be a done deal. A trade row is {offered_entry, received_entry, response_time, tradeitem_set: [{element_in, element_out}]}: the offering side receives each element_in and gives each element_out (checked against who owns the players now on 2026-10-18). Entry IDs are the same team IDs the rest of the app uses.
- A deal counts from the first gameweek whose deadline is after it was agreed (first_gameweek_after, from response_time) up to the latest finished gameweek. A gameweek whose points can't be read is skipped, and a gameweek in progress is never counted, so nobody gets teased over points still to be scored.
- Judged on raw points (build_trade): each side's players received against the players given up, summed over those gameweeks. The two swings are exact opposites. deal_size() sets a swing against luck: one player's points in a gameweek stray PLAYER_POINTS_SD (3.5, the recap's number) from his average, so luck = 3.5 x the square root of (players in the deal x gameweeks). A swing is a "win" from DEAL_WIN_SIGMAS (1.5) x luck and at least DEAL_MIN_SWING (6), a "robbery" from DEAL_ROBBERY_SIGMAS (2.5) x luck, and otherwise "even" (shown as too close to call). On the two real trades the swing was 4 after 2 gameweeks against luck of 15.7 and 9.9: even.
- state is "waiting" (no finished gameweek since), "even" or "decided" (winner and loser are team IDs). Each side gets a label for the tracker: before DEAL_SETTLED_WEEKS (4) finished gameweeks "Ahead so far", "Behind so far" or "Too close to call"; from 4 "Worth it", "Regretted" or "A wash". "running" is the offering side's cumulative swing after each gameweek.
- Lines (trade_lines): a decided trade gets a praise line for the winner and a poke for the loser (PRAISE_WIN, PRAISE_ROBBERY, POKE_WIN, POKE_ROBBERY); an even one gets a single line (EVEN_DEALS). Which template is picked comes from the trade's ID (choose()), so a deal always gets the same wording. The pokes aim at the deal, never at the person, like the rest of the banter. Team names are marked with mark(), and every number carries its unit (points_word()). "detail" names the best scorer in the deal and who has him now.
- The recap card (league_deals "items"): trades with 1 to DEAL_NEWS_WEEKS (3) finished gameweeks, newest first, at most DEALS_SHOWN (3); older trades are only in the tracker. After them come Pickup of the week, Dropped and regretted and Everyone missed this one.
- Pickups and drops (build_moves, standout_moves, move_lines): the accepted waiver ("w") and free-agent ("f") claims in /transactions whose first gameweek is the latest finished one, each the player claimed against the player dropped for him over that one gameweek. The biggest gain is the pickup and the biggest loss the regretted drop, each only if it is at least MOVE_MIN_SWING (6) points. They are fun superlatives, not a verdict, so they don't have to beat luck the way a trade does.
- Everyone missed this one (missed_players): the best rated free agent (the Next 5 view, from load_league) that nobody bid on, if he rates MISSED_MIN_RATING (65) or more, and the free agent who scored most in the latest finished gameweek, if that was MISSED_HAUL_POINTS (10) or more (never the same player twice). A bid is any transaction with him as element_in in the last MISSED_DAYS (7) days, denied ones included.
- The page only draws what the server sent (dealItem and tradeCard in league.html; rich() bolds the marked team names). fillDealCard() loads the card on its own when the Recap is opened, so a problem there never hides the recap (the card is just hidden).

## How league banter works
- league_banter(details) in app.py, route /api/league/<id>/banter. Built from the league details (matches and league_history's table), so no extra requests. None until a gameweek has finished or if the league has no matches.
- The three cards are three different matches from the next unplayed gameweek (nobody is on two cards, and nothing from later weeks): hot_match = the pair with the lowest table places added together (then the smaller gap); Battle for 3rd = of the rest, the pair whose places are closest to 3rd; Wooden spoon watch = of the rest, the lowest-placed pair. A card is left out if the gameweek has too few matches. Each has a "line" saying how far apart they are and a "tease" (CARD_TEASES: three wordings for each kind of card, picked by the gameweek).
- facts (in this order, first FACT_COUNT = 5 that apply): luck (table place at least LUCK_MIN_GAP = 2 below points-scored place), harsh (highest score that still lost), streak (current winning or losing run of STREAK_MIN = 3 or more), thrashing, closest, low, high. The page shows 2, the rest behind "Show more". Text is written from the numbers, playful teasing a notch sharper than mild (it pokes at the result or the decision, never at the person), no AI.
- Wording (BANTER_LINES): every kind of fact has three wordings, and banter_wording() picks one with choose() from the latest finished gameweek plus the letters of the kind, so a fact reads the same all gameweek, differently the next, and different kinds of fact don't all switch together. Placeholders are filled by say() inside league_banter; names go in already marked with mark(). A test fills in every wording of every kind from real fake leagues, so a wording that asks for a field it isn't given fails the tests rather than the page.
- "week": {gw, facts} is the same kind of fact about the latest finished gameweek only (wk_high, wk_low, wk_win, wk_close or wk_draw, wk_climb, wk_fall). The closest match is never the same match as the biggest win (a lone match is only the biggest win), a draw counts as the closest, and a climb or fall needs WEEK_MOVE_MIN (2) places (places_at, the same table order the recap uses; none in the first gameweek). Ties for the best or worst score go to the better-placed team. The page shows all of them, because they are the part that changes weekly.
- Last season's facts (head-to-head records between managers across seasons, finish vs last year) are not built yet: checked on 2026-09-30: the Draft API only returns the current season. The league (52607) is marked is_renewed and keeps the same ID and entry IDs, but its team history, matches and standings start again at GW1, and a season parameter is ignored. Player totals for past seasons exist on the classic site (element-summary history_past, matched by player code), but nothing about managers. So this needs last season's results entered by hand (e.g. a final standings screenshot), or snapshots saved from this season on.
- Records and scoring (2026-11-06): league_banter(details, snapshots=()) takes the saved seasons plus this one (the banter route passes season_snapshots, the same merge Rivalries uses) and each card gets a "record" from pair_record(): {won, drawn, lost, meetings, line}, from the first manager's side, with one sentence in line (names marked): "First meeting on record.", "Met once, in GW3: X won 45-38.", or for several meetings the series and the latest result. A meeting from an earlier season than the newest snapshot names its season. record is None when no snapshots are passed, so a caller without them doesn't claim "first meeting". It also returns "scoring": every manager in table order with points_for, points_against and league_points over their finished matches, which the points scored and conceded chart (now at the bottom of Banter) draws.

## How the pinned match-ups work
- The three cards sit above the hub's choice buttons (section #matchups, outside every panel), so they show whichever choice is open and stay when you switch. They are drawn from the banter data, which the page now asks for as soon as a league opens (refreshBanter in league.html); Banter reuses the same data. The strip is hidden when there are no cards (before a gameweek finishes, a classic league or the end of the season).
- Live scores: load_matchups_live(league_id), route /api/league/<id>/live, returns {live: None} or {live: {gw, scores: {team ID: {points, left, playing}}}} for the managers on the cards. It is only worked out when a game of the cards' gameweek has kicked off (from the classic fixtures feed, via gameweek_games), and needs the live points feed and the managers' picks (recap_inputs, for just those managers: 1 + up to 6 requests, cached 10 minutes like the recap's) and then live_scores, the recap's own maths. It needs no ratings, so it never loads the league. If any feed is down it returns None and the cards just show the usual line. The page shows the score in place of the "v", a Live tag and "N v M players still to play. An estimate until the gameweek ends." (auto-subs and unconfirmed bonus points aren't in it, like the recap).
- Freshness: the page asks again every MATCH_REFRESH_MS (5 minutes) while the tab is visible and when a background tab comes back after longer than that. Banter is asked again each time it is opened but not within BANTER_FRESH_MS (15 seconds) of the last request, and two callers at once share one request. The server still keeps the league for 10 minutes, so most of these asks are answered from its cache.

## How side battles and the club market work
- side_battles(gw, fixtures, owned, matches, teams) in app.py, loaded by load_battles(league_id) for the route /api/league/<id>/battles, drawn by renderBattles() (the Banter section "Who's cheering for whom"), battleLine() (the pinned cards) and drawMyMatches() (the Next up card of Your matches). It is for the first gameweek with unfinished matches, the same one the cards at the top are for, which can be the gameweek being played and not the Draft site's "next". So the fixtures can't come from the players (their fixtures start at the next gameweek): they come from the classic fixtures feed through gameweek_games, one entry for each fixture (the home side's view, so each is counted once), in kick-off order. Who owns each player is one request (element-status), and a manager's players are their whole squad of 15, best first (by season points) for the hover text, the same as the club grid. It needs no ratings, so it never loads the league. battles is None when every match is finished or the fixtures feed can't be read, and the page then shows nothing. A failure of the route only hides the section and the lines: the cards and the rest of Banter don't wait for it.
- Returns {gw, min, fixtures, matches}: min is BATTLE_MIN. Each fixture has home_owners and away_owners ({entry_id, team, n, players}, the biggest stake first) and battles (the pairs fighting there, {a, b} team names). Each match has a, b (team IDs), a_team, b_team, battles (how many fixtures they fight over; a double gameweek counts each fixture) and biggest ({home, away, a_club, a_n, b_club, b_n}, or None) with a line (names marked for bold) about it. The biggest battle is the one where the smaller side has the most players, then the one with the most players in all, then by club name so it is the same every time. Both ways round count (the first manager can cheer for the home side or the away side), and the line reads the same whichever manager is listed first.
- Wording: BATTLE_LINES has three wordings, picked by banter_wording() from the gameweek like every other banter line.
- The club market: club_market(transactions, club_of, week) in app.py, loaded by load_market(league_id) for the route /api/league/<id>/market (the players for each one's club, the league details for its name and the Draft site's claims through draft_rows: three requests, no points), drawn by renderMarket() under the club grid (marketBlock, asked for by loadMarket() next to loadClubs()). A claim counts when it is kind "w" (waiver) or "f" (free agent) and accepted (result "a"): the club of the player claimed (element_in) gets an "in" and the club of the player dropped for him (element_out) an "out". Trades, denied claims and players the list doesn't know don't count (the claim itself is still in "claims"). Returns {claims, clubs, lines}: clubs are those with at least one in or out, most net first (ties: most "in", then club code), each {club, bought, sold, net}; lines are banter ({text}, three wordings each in MARKET_LINES, picked by the current gameweek): the club with the most net "in" when it is MARKET_MIN_NET (3) or more, the club with the most net "out" when it is that far below zero, and when neither is, a line about the biggest swing ("A quiet market"). Nothing when there are no claims.
- The page draws a diverging bar chart in plain SVG: one row for each club, a bar to the right (green) for more in than out and to the left (red) for the other way round, with "+6 (11 in, 5 out)" beside it, because a club with equal ins and outs has a net of 0 but is still busy. The axis fits the biggest swing, with the same distance either side of 0.

## How squad DNA and where the points are work
- Squad DNA is drawn from the same data as the club grid (club_exposure, route /api/league/<id>/clubs): each manager now also carries `clubs_n` (how many different clubs) and `stacks` ([{club, n}], the biggest stack first, ties by club code). The managers' order is the biggest stack first, then the fewest clubs, then the team's name (the fewest-clubs step is new: without it Mandems fantasy, with three stacks of three, came fourth behind teams that only have one). The page (renderClubs, with showClubs for the switch) draws one bar for each manager who has players: a flex row where each segment's width is its number of players, so every bar is the same length (15 players). Segments are buttons (class dn1, dn2 or dn3 for one, two or three or more players), and clicking one calls pickClub, the same state (clubsPick) and the same "N from CLUB: players" line as the grid. A club with no game next gameweek gets a red dot and one that plays twice an amber one, from the same flags as the grid headers (nothing is flagged when the fixture data doesn't cover that gameweek). On a phone the name sits above the bar, and one-player segments drop their club code because they're too narrow (the title and a tap still say which club).
- Where the points are: club_points(elements, owner_of, short, week) in app.py, loaded by load_club_points(league_id) for the route /api/league/<id>/club-points, drawn by renderWire() (asked for by loadWire() next to loadClubs(), on its own, so a failure only hides the block). It reads the player list (each player's club and season points) and who owns each player (element-status): two requests, and nothing else. For each club it adds up the season points of the players owned in the league (owned, owned_points) and of free agents (wire_points), the best free agent (the top scorer on the wire, ties by name, none if nobody there has scored) and points per owned player. Clubs are sorted by wire points, most first (ties by code). A player's season total includes games before he was dropped, so "on the wire" is what those players have scored this season, and the page says so under the heading.
- Lines (POINTS_LINES, three wordings each, picked by the gameweek like BANTER_LINES): the club with the most points on the wire (when there are any), then the best and worst club to own by points per owned player, only among clubs with CLUB_POINTS_MIN_OWNED (4) or more owned players (a club with one lucky owned player would otherwise win) and only when at least two clubs qualify. Clubs go by their three-letter code.
- The page shows the first WIRE_SHOWN (8) clubs with a "Show all 20 clubs" button, and a bar for each club split into owned and wire points, all on the same scale (the club with the most points in all fills the width).

## How season odds work
- season_odds(details, strengths, runs) in app.py, route /api/league/<id>/odds (get_odds keeps the result per league, set of results and day, so it is simulated once until a result changes or the day does; at most ODDS_KEPT (20) kept, behind a lock), drawn by renderOdds() in static/league.html (the Odds choice). It needs the league details (finished matches and the whole fixture list, which the Draft site serves for all 38 gameweeks) and, for the squad share, squad_strengths(league_id): load_league on the "Until the break" view (a longer view than Next 5, which suits a whole season), the managers who have a best eleven. That loads the whole league (about 4 seconds cold), so it is only done once there are ODDS_MIN_WEEKS (3) finished gameweeks, and if it fails the odds go without the squad share (the share goes to form). The day is in the cache key because squads change daily. None before 3 finished gameweeks or for a league without matches.
- The model: each manager's weekly score is a normal curve. Its middle is odds_estimates(): 70% form, 20% squad and 10% table. Form is their scores so far with a week losing half its weight for every ODDS_HALF_LIFE (4) gameweeks it is old, pulled towards the league average by ODDS_PRIOR_WEEKS (5) weeks' worth (so with recent weeks counting more, fewer weeks count and it is pulled harder). Squad is their squad's strength rating turned into points a week: ODDS_SQUAD_POINTS (0.3) for each point above or below the average squad, added to the league average. Table is their win rate (a draw is half a win, nudged off 0 and 1 by adding one win and one loss) turned into the weekly score that would win that often against the league (league average + spread x the square root of 2 x the normal curve's inverse of the win rate). The shares are ODDS_SQUAD_WEIGHT (0.2) and ODDS_TABLE_WEIGHT (0.1), the rest is form; a manager with no squad rating, or any league with fewer than two, gets form and table only (a rating of 0 means no eleven and is ignored). The spread is one number for everybody, the league's typical week-to-week spread (floor ODDS_MIN_SD). In every simulated season each manager's true average is also nudged by a random amount with a spread of ODDS_STRENGTH_SD (4) points, because nine weeks can't pin down how good a squad is and squads change: without that the leader showed 93% for the title at GW9, with it about 60-70%. Every match still to play is decided on two sampled scores (rounded, so a draw is possible), 3 points for a win and 1 for a draw (WIN_POINTS, DRAW_POINTS), on top of the real table, with points scored as the tie-break, like the real table. The random numbers are seeded from the latest finished gameweek, the matches left and the number of runs (a string seed, which Python hashes the same way every time), so the same results always give the same odds.
- Returns {runs, gws_played, gws_left, weights, managers, lines}: weights are the shares actually used (form, squad, table; squad is 0 when no ratings could be used), managers are in table order, each with place, league_points, average (the weekly score the simulation used), odds (the chance of each place, 1st first: each row adds to 1 and each place's column adds to 1 across the managers, give or take rounding to four decimals), win, top3, last, expected_place and why; lines are the headline sentences (names marked for bold). percent_text() writes a chance as a whole percent, "under 1%" below 1%.
- The page draws a grid of every manager against every finishing place, shaded by chance (the Spoon column in red), your row in bold, and a note written from the weights the server used (it says when the squad ratings couldn't be loaded). Selecting a row opens its "why" (odds_why(): the server's lines on form, squad, luck and the weekly score assumed; trend is "rising" or "slumping" when the latest ODDS_TREND_WEEKS (4) weeks differ from the weeks before by ODDS_TREND_GAP (8) points a week, which also puts a small arrow beside the name; there are no trend lines until a manager has 6 scores) and, once the moves have arrived, a "Best move so far" line from the moves data. The moves request (askMoves) is shared with Trades and moves, asked for after the grid is drawn so the grid isn't held up by the slower feed. Opened rows stay open when the table is drawn again. When no matches are left it says the season is over and the grid is the final table. Cost on the real league: about 0.9 seconds of simulation for 4,000 seasons, plus about 4 seconds the first time for the squads.
- How the odds have moved: odds_history(league_id, details, runs) in app.py, route /api/league/<id>/odds-history, drawn by renderMoved() under the grid (asked for once per league, after the grid is drawn, by loadMoved()). For each finished gameweek from ODDS_MIN_WEEKS (3) on it calls odds_as_of(details, gw, runs): season_odds on a copy of the league in which every match after that gameweek is marked unplayed (the real details aren't changed), with no squads. Each is ODDS_HISTORY_RUNS (1,000) seasons, fewer than the grid's 4,000, because there are many; the result for a past gameweek can't change, so it is kept in memory (ODDS_PAST_KEPT (400), one per league and gameweek, under its own lock) and after the first call only a new gameweek costs anything (about 0.3 seconds each; a cold call late in the season could take about 10). Returns {runs, gws, managers}: managers in table order now, each with win, top3 and last, one chance (0 to 1) for each gameweek; the title chances of all managers add to 1 at every gameweek. Each point is its own simulation, so lines wiggle a point or two. None before 3 finished gameweeks. The page's switch (Title, Top 3, Wooden spoon) only changes which list is drawn; the y axis fits the biggest chance on that chart; the end of the line is labelled for whoever leads on that chance, and for you.

## How moves work
- league_moves(trades, transactions, names, teams, deadlines, finished, points) in app.py, loaded by load_moves(league_id) for the route /api/league/<id>/moves, drawn by renderActivity() and drawActivity() (ids start with `activity`, because the Recap already has a movesSection) in the Trades and moves choice. It reads the same Draft feeds as Deals (trades, transactions, finished gameweeks' live points) but is its own route, so the Recap's deals card, which asks for only the gameweeks a trade covers, stays quick. The page asks for it from renderDeals (fillActivity), on its own, so a problem there never hides the trade tracker.
- What counts: every accepted claim (kind "w" waiver or "f" free agent, result "a") and every processed trade (state "p"). A claim is judged on the claimed player's points against the player dropped for him, over the finished gameweeks from the first one after the claim until the same manager dropped him again (a later accepted claim whose element_out is him ends the window; if that leaves no gameweek, the claim is not judged) or the latest finished gameweek; a gameweek whose points can't be read is skipped, and a claim for a gameweek that hasn't finished is counted as "waiting" and shown in the headline. The dropped player is always the same position (Draft squads are fixed), so the points compare. A trade is judged by build_trade, each side's swing, and counts as one move for each side. Raw points, whether or not the player started.
- Returns {gameweek, claims, trades, waiting, average_claim, headline, managers, best, worst, lines}: managers are everyone, most net points first and managers with no moves last, each with moves, claims, trades, net, per_move (None with none) and their best and worst single move; best and worst are the biggest and smallest single moves in the league (MOVES_BEST_SHOWN (3) of each, the worst only if it lost points). The headline says whether claims have beaten the player dropped on average (a gap of 1 point or more either way, otherwise "about as well"). lines are banter, three wordings each (MOVE_LINES, picked by the gameweek like BANTER_LINES): the top manager, the sharpest (best points a move among those with MOVES_SHARP_MIN (3) moves), the busiest if they are below the league's average claim, the one most in the red, and everyone with no moves.
- Real league on 2026-11-06 (GW9): 75 claims judged and 5 waiting, an average of +6.8 points a claim, every manager positive on claims; two managers had made no moves at all and were 6th and last.

## How your matches and the season awards work
- The server never learns who anyone is. /api/league/<id>/people (league_people: every manager {entry_id, team, manager}, by manager name; light, it needs only the league details) feeds the pop-up and is asked as soon as the hub opens, and /api/league/<id>/upcoming returns everyone's remaining matches when Your matches is opened; the page picks out the chosen manager. Choosing a name writes the same localStorage key (teamId) the main page uses, so the main page and the Charts highlight follow it. The address never carries it, and setAddress also leaves out tab=upcoming so a link copied after the page opened on Your matches opens on the choices. The page: loadPeople() (from openLeague) fills PEOPLE and asks (askWho) unless the browser's teamId is one of them or they chose Just looking this session (sessionStorage, per league); chooseMe(), rememberSkip() and youChanged() do the rest, and renderUpcoming()/drawMyMatches() draw the card.
- league_upcoming(details, strengths, snapshots) (None before a gameweek finishes or for a classic league): managers in table order {entry_id, team, manager, position, league_points, form (last FORM_LENGTH (5) results, oldest first), strength}; upcoming {team ID: [{gw, opponent, live, edge}]} soonest first (every match that isn't finished, "live" when the match has started); head_to_head {team ID: {opponent, won, drawn, lost, last}} against their next opponent. edge is favourite/underdog when the squads' best-eleven strengths (Next 5 view) differ by EDGE_MIN_STRENGTH (2.0) or more, otherwise even, and None when a strength is missing.
- The upcoming route loads the league once (load_league, week view) for the strengths. That is optional: if it fails the card still shows who's next, places and form, just no strengths or verdict. Records come from season_snapshots (saved seasons plus this season's live results, the same merge Rivalries uses).
- season_awards(played, names, place, luck) returned as "awards" in the banter data: [] until AWARD_MIN_WEEKS (3) gameweeks have finished, and only managers with that many matches are considered (and at least two of them). Points machine = highest average score, Fortress = lowest average conceded, Mr Consistent = smallest spread (standard deviation) of weekly scores, Boom or Bust = largest spread, Unluckiest = the bottom of the luck chart when it is LUCK_FACT_MIN (2) points or more below. Ties go to the better table place. Each award is {kind, title, entry_id, team, detail}; the page marks your own in amber.

## How saved seasons and rivalries work
- The Draft API only returns the current season and wipes a league's results when it renews, so we keep our own copy. snapshot_league(details) in app.py turns the finished matches into a small record (season label, managers, and each match as gw, a, b, a_points, b_points using team IDs, which stay the same across seasons). Season label comes from the date (a season starts in July, SEASON_STARTS_IN), e.g. "2026-27".
- save_snapshot.py <league id> writes it to history/<season>/league_<id>.json. save_snapshot() only writes when there are more finished matches than the saved file has, so running it often is harmless and a reset league can never overwrite a fuller record.
- .github/workflows/snapshot.yml runs it every day at 06:00 UTC for league 52607 and commits the file if it changed (can also be run by hand from the Actions tab). The first snapshot (30 matches, GW1-5 of 2026-27) was saved by hand on 2026-10-01.
- league_rivalries(details, league_id, a, b) combines the saved files with this season's live results (live replaces the saved copy of the same season), and league_rivalry() lists every meeting between two managers plus their record. Route: /api/league/<id>/rivalries?a=&b= (without a and b you only get the manager list).
- The Rivalries choice in the league hub (view-only link too) has two pickers, a record summary, a bar chart of the points margin in each meeting (green = first manager won) and a list of every meeting.

## How the luck chart works
- Part of Banter, not Charts (league_banter in app.py). expected_league_points(history) works out, for each week a manager played a real match, how many league points they'd have got playing everyone (3 per win, 1 per draw, averaged over the other managers who had a score that week), and adds the weeks up. league_banter returns "luck": every manager, luckiest first, with league_points, expected_points and luck (= league_points minus expected, 1 decimal; positive = lucky). The page draws bars: green right of zero, red left.
- Two banter facts use it: "unlucky" (the manager furthest below their deserved points) and "lucky" (furthest above), each only if the gap is at least LUCK_FACT_MIN = 2 league points. The older "luck" fact (table place vs points-scored place) is skipped when "unlucky" already names the same team. FACT_COUNT is now 9 so no fact is cut; the page still shows 2 at first.
- Byes are skipped so it compares with the same weeks as the real points. Luck doesn't sum to zero across the league, because a draw is worth 2 points in total in the "play everyone" table but a win is worth 3.

## How the weekly recap works
- Route /api/league/<id>/recap -> load_recap(league_id) in app.py, drawn by the Recap choice in static/league.html (/league?league=<id>&tab=recap). The page only draws what the server sends (text, label, table moves as bullets, scoreboard or results, and stars while the games are on) and asks again each time the choice is opened. recap is None before any game has finished and for a league without matches (classic scoring).
- Which gameweek and stage: gw = the Draft site's "current" event. gameweek_games() reads that gameweek's games from the classic fixtures feed (kickoff, started, finished or finished_provisional: a game counts as played at full time, before bonus points are confirmed). gameweek_progress() counts games played and match days (UTC calendar days of kickoff) that are completely done. recap_stage(): "final" once every match of the gameweek is finished in the league details; otherwise the number of completed match days (0 = the first games are done but no whole day yet), at most RECAP_MAX_LIVE_STAGES (4); None while no game has finished. With no stage yet, last week's final recap is shown (or nothing in GW1).
- A recap is only written when the stage changes, which gives about 3-5 a gameweek: with a Friday opener it's Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday, then final; without one it's an early look after the first Saturday games, then each day, then final. Under RECAP_SHORT_SHARE (25%) of the games played the recap is "short" (an early look, 2-3 sentences); otherwise 2-3 short paragraphs. A lone Friday game is 1 of 10, so it gets the short one.
- Live scores: recap_inputs() fetches /event/<gw>/live (points and minutes per player) and every manager's picks from /entry/<id>/event/<gw> (places 1-11 start, 12-15 are the bench), cached like everything else. live_scores() adds up each starting eleven (auto-subs aren't counted: the Draft site makes them when the gameweek ends, so it's an estimate), counts the starters whose club game isn't finished (left) and in progress (playing), and works out the points those players should still add from points per game (a player part-way through his only game counts for the share of 90 minutes he has left). A star (shown only while the games are on) needs STAR_MIN_POINTS (6).
- Comeback outlook: outlook() says "level", "all but over" (the side behind has nobody left to play) or, from comeback_chance(), one of COMEBACK_LEVELS: 35% or more "wide open", 12% "still alive", 3% "a long shot", else "needs a miracle". The chance is a normal curve: the gap to make up is the margin plus what the leader's remaining players should add minus what the trailing side's should add, against a spread of PLAYER_POINTS_SD (3.5) times the square root of the players left on both sides. A guide, not a prediction. The words are worked out here and the AI is told to use them as given.
- recap_facts() builds the bundle everything is written from: state, length, games played, the matches (with outlook), the table's top three and last place, highlights, and, while the games are on, stars. Highlights (recap_highlights()) are the talking points: for a final recap the biggest win, the closest of the other matches (a draw counts), and the highest and lowest score; while the games are on (live=True) the biggest lead, the closest match and the highest score so far (no lowest, because a low score so far may just be a side that hasn't played yet). The closest match needs a second match and the scores need two matches that differ. For a final recap only: table moves (at least MOVE_MIN_PLACES places, up to three) and up to three banter facts. A final recap has no stars and no next-up. The written text deliberately doesn't repeat the results, the moves or (live) each match's outlook and story, because the page lists them right below it (the AI is told the same). Team and league names go through clean_name() (control characters out, 40 characters) because the managers choose them, and the prompt says names and facts are data, never instructions.
- Match stories (final recap only, add_stories()): each finished match gets tags, a basis and a players line, and each side its table place going into the gameweek (places_at() on the league history, so none in the first gameweek) and its squad strength. Strength is each manager's best-eleven strength from load_league() (week view), which is the squads as they are now, not when the match was played; if that can't be loaded the strength reason is simply left out. Upset = the winner sat UPSET_MIN_PLACES or more places below the loser, or was UPSET_MIN_STRENGTH or more weaker: either is enough and the basis lists which. Stomping/Nail-biter come from the margin (STOMPING_MARGIN, NAILBITER_MARGIN); a draw is tagged Draw and is never an upset. The players line comes from team_players() (live points and picks, starters only: top two scorers, and a flop = played and got FLOP_MAX_POINTS or fewer) and names the winner's top scorers, the loser's best and the loser's flop. Players' own points are shown and never summed, because the official match totals include auto-subs and bonus points.
- Live match stories (live_story() in recap_matches, live recaps only): live_scores() adds to each side `top` (the two highest scorers so far, those on at least 1 point) and `coming` (the two best-rated starters whose club game isn't finished, each with opponent, home/away, fixture difficulty, whether the game is on now, and `standout`). gameweek_games() now gives each club's opponent short name, home/away and difficulty (from the classic fixtures' team_h_difficulty and team_a_difficulty) as well as the kickoff and finished flags. Ratings are each player's score from load_league() (the Next 5 view), which load_recap() now calls for live and final recaps alike. The story is written in Python so it shows without the AI; the AI is told to tell the story of the day from the highlights, pick the two or three most interesting matches, not repeat the story lines the page shows under its text, and only mention players in the facts.
- Writing: write_recap() sends RECAP_SYSTEM and recap_request(facts) to the Anthropic Messages API (the anthropic SDK; no streaming; effort RECAP_EFFORT "low"; a 30 second timeout so it fits inside gunicorn's 60 seconds; no retries). RECAP_MODEL (environment variable, no default in the code) names the model, and unset means no AI. An SDK error (or the SDK's TypeError when no key is set), a refusal, a cut-off answer or an empty answer returns None and plain_recap() is used instead. The failure is logged ("The AI recap failed (...)") in Render's logs.
- get_recap() keeps each AI recap in memory by (league, gameweek, stage), so the first visitor after a stage change waits and everyone else reads it; a lock stops two visitors paying for the same one. After a failed call the AI isn't tried again for that stage for RECAP_RETRY_SECONDS (5 minutes). Only leagues in RECAP_LEAGUES (52607) get AI recaps; any other league gets the plain one, so nobody can run up the bill by trying other league IDs. At most RECAP_KEPT (60) recaps are kept.
- plain_recap() is the same facts as sentences, so the page works with no AI at all. For a final recap it's the highlights, one to a line ("Biggest win: A beat B 90-50, by 40 points.", "Closest match: ...", "Highest score: ... Lowest: ..."); while the games are on it's the talking points so far, one to a line ("Biggest lead: A lead B 45-31, by 14 points.", "Closest: ...", "Highest score so far: ..."; an early look with few games played gives just the first). The page keeps single line breaks in the text. Each match's outlook and story line are only in the scoreboard under it, so the text and the scoreboard no longer say the same thing.

## Decisions
- Back in play is a list next to the ratings, not a change to them (agreed 2026-11-10). Why: the normal rating answers "who is good right now", and it should stay low for a player who hasn't kicked a ball, or the waiver cards and the lineup would start recommending players who may not start for weeks. So a returning player gets two numbers, now and when fit, and nothing else on the page moves. Cost: the waiver cards, Your team this week and the trade analyzer still judge him on his rating now (see Known issues).
- The rating when fit is the normal formula on adjusted numbers, measured with the real players' yardstick (2026-11-10). Why: it can then be set beside any other rating on the page, and it needs no second formula to keep in step with the first. My first try rated everyone again with the made-up lines mixed in, and that moved other players' ratings by up to 0.9, because the made-up lines shifted the 95th percentile; passing the yardstick in fixed it. Cost: score_players has one more optional argument (`scale`), and its first loop moved into raw_stats().
- "Has been flagged this season" is what separates coming back from not being picked (2026-11-10, from the real feed). Fit with no points lately also describes a player his club has dropped: Senesi (175 points last season, 178 minutes this season, never flagged) and Saliba (flagged 23 Jul, no minutes) look the same without it. The feed says when the latest news was written (news_added) but not when it was cleared (news_updated is empty for everyone), so the app can say he was flagged, not when he was passed fit.
- The pre-season draft rank only decides who gets a past-season lookup; it is not the "big name" test (2026-11-10). Why: the bar on the rating when fit already decides who is worth a heads-up, on numbers, and the rank is one request cheaper than checking everyone. The Draft feed's draft_rank looks like the pre-season ranking (Haaland 1, Saka 3; Konsa is ranked 452 on 57 points), though it isn't documented. Cost: a player with under 180 minutes this season and a rank outside the top 180 is never listed, whatever he did last season.
- The bars are 60 when fit and 10 above his rating now (first guesses, set on 2026-11-10's data): 60 kept Mitoma (61) and dropped Sávio (55) and Rashford (45, on 352 poor minutes this season); the gap of 10 is there so a player the normal rating already has about right isn't listed twice.
- A doubt with no return date (50% to play) goes under Back soon, and the third group is called "Out for now" (2026-11-10, both small changes from the design as agreed, which said "out, with a return date" and "Out, no date yet"). Why: a 50% doubt is not out, and the third group also holds players with a return date more than 3 gameweeks away. The waiver cards never suggest anyone under 75%, so this is the one place a good 50% doubt shows up.
- past_seasons() was split in two (2026-11-10): classic_history() fetches the classic site's rows and past_seasons() shortens them to season, points and minutes as before. Why: the rating when fit needs the whole row (starts, expected goals and the rest), and two copies of the fetching code would drift apart.
- League race and Places over time are one section with a switch, not two charts (agreed 2026-11-08). Why: they draw the same data: the table order after each gameweek is just the league-points order (checked on the real league: 0 of 9 gameweeks differed; only the points-scored tie-break is extra). Points shows how far apart managers are, which Places can't, and Places shows who overtook whom, which the points lines hide where they tie (100 tied pairs across the 9 gameweeks, 30 after GW1), so both are worth keeping, but not as two sections saying the same thing. Cost: you see one view at a time. The switch opens on Points (what it always showed).
- The odds-over-time chart uses the same simplified model for every gameweek (agreed and built 2026-11-08): scores and league results, no squads. Why: squad ratings were only saved from GW6, so a model with squads would change in the middle of the chart and the line would jump for a reason that isn't about the league; the same model all the way through shows only how the results moved the odds. Cost: its last point is not the grid's number (64% against 58% for R Sanchez at GW9), which looks like a mistake until the note under the chart is read. If it grates, from the next gameweek the daily job could save the real odds each day (like the strengths) and the chart could switch to those, keeping this version only for the weeks before.
- Who owns which club is on the Charts page, and counts the squad of 15 (agreed 2026-11-08). Why: asked for there; the squad is what a blank gameweek hurts, since a benched player can still be needed, and it needs no ratings. The league it was built for is well spread (the most from one club is 3), so most of the time the chart says that nobody is all-in; it earns its place in blank and double gameweeks. League race and Places over time were merged afterwards, which made room on Charts (see Status).
- "Head to head at the top alongside the week's match-up" means the pairing's record on each card (agreed 2026-11-06, by building it and saying so; the other reading was the whole grid pinned on top). Why: the grid is 12 columns wide and mostly 1-0-0 until the second round of fixtures (GW12), and pinning it above every choice would push everything down on a phone; the record answers the question the card asks (what happened last time they met). The grid itself went to the top of Rivalries, not away. Cost: if the grid pinned on top was what was meant, it is one move back.
- Odds v2 blends recent form, squad strength and a little of the table, and keeps them small (agreed 2026-11-08, after a backtest). Why: asked for "lean more on league points, base it on recent performances, blend in squad strength". I tested each idea on the league's own data, predicting every manager's score for GW4-9 from only the weeks before it (72 predictions; the average miss in points, lower is better): guessing the league average 10.65; scores so far 9.82; recent weeks counting more 9.80 (a tie); adding 10% league points 9.96, 30% 10.26, 50% 10.63 (barely better than guessing). Shrinking towards the league average by 5 weeks was the best of 0-20. Squad strength (from the saved daily snapshots, 48 manager-weeks, GW6-9) correlated +0.23 with the week's score (Next 5 view) against +0.33 for the recent average, and added nothing once the recent average was known (+0.00). So: recent weeks are free, a modest squad share is worth it because it is the only thing that sees a transfer before it reaches the scores (and the saved strengths are too few to say it is useless), and league points only get a small nod because they carry luck (which opponent you drew): the more weight there, the worse the guesses, so 10% is a taste, not a belief. Steezar FC (12 points, 4th highest scorer) stays above goblin gang (15 points, 6th in the table) even at 25% table weight, because the gap in scoring (41 against 35 a week) is bigger than the table can close; the "why" notes are how that gets explained. The three weights are named settings. Cost: the squad share needs the whole league loaded (about 4 seconds), and the ratings are a first guess.
- A good transfer reaches the odds through recent scores and the squad rating, and is shown in the "why" (agreed 2026-11-08, for the "De Cuyper" idea: someone's pickup is working). It is not a separate boost, because it would count the same points twice: the player's points are already in the recent scores and he is already in the squad rating. Formation changes are not included: the feeds only give a manager's formation through their picks for every gameweek (about 108 requests), the same data the bench regret idea needs, so they belong with that.
- Moves are judged against the player dropped, on raw points, with the window ending when the claimed player is dropped again (agreed 2026-11-06). Why: the same measure as Deals (so the two agree), needs nothing recorded, and the "dropped again" rule stops a player's points after he left the squad counting for the claim that brought him (one claim in the real league, Ajayi for Mitchell, was dropped again before it ever counted and is left out: 76 accepted claims, 75 judged). Cost: it flatters claims, because the player dropped is often injured or on the bench and scores nothing: the real league's average claim was +6.8 and every manager was positive, so the interesting part is how much, not whether. The page says so. A measure on the points a claimed player scored while starting would need every manager's picks for every gameweek (the bench regret pipeline).
- Odds and Moves each have their own route, and the Odds a choice of its own (agreed 2026-11-06). Why: the hub choices are where the page keeps a heavy thing out of the way until it is asked for; the odds need a second of simulation and the moves need up to 38 live feeds, and neither should slow the Recap's deals card, the banter or the Charts. Cost: eight choices with My team on the full version.
- The match-ups are pinned above the choices, not stuck to the top of the screen (agreed 2026-11-06). Why: the ask was that they stay visible "despite browsing different tabs"; above the choice buttons they are on every choice and cost nothing when you scroll, where a sticky strip would cover a third of a phone screen. Cost: they scroll away with the page, and on a phone the manager names and the tease are hidden to keep the three rows slim.
- Live scores on the cards are a bonus that loads second (agreed 2026-11-06). Why: the cards should appear at once from data the page already has, and scores need the live feeds (several requests). So /banter and /live are separate, and a failure in /live only means the cards show the old line. Cost: a card can show for a moment without its score.
- "Update more regularly" meant new content, not only a refresh (agreed 2026-11-06). Why: every fact was a season record ("biggest beating so far"), and records only change when somebody breaks one, so after nine gameweeks the same lines sat there for weeks; reloading the data couldn't help. So there is a section about the latest gameweek only, three wordings per fact picked by the gameweek, and Banter is asked for again when reopened. Cost: the wording rotates on a fixed cycle of three, so after three weeks a fact repeats its sentence (about the new numbers); and no AI wording, for the reasons the other banter uses templates.
- The banter went up another notch (agreed 2026-11-06) but still pokes at the result or the decision and never at the person: lines like "That's not bad luck, that's a crime scene" and "Even the autopilot would have done better", not lines about a manager as a person. It is a friends' league; the rule keeps it that way.
- Places over time is a bump chart because the league race already shows points (agreed 2026-11-06). Why: places are what people argue about, lines cross where places change, and it needed nothing new (the table order is already worked out for the recap's table moves). Cost: a manager who is a long way clear still looks like one line among twelve; the race shows the size of the gap, this shows the order. Alternatives not built: luck over time, and squad strength against points scored.
- Strength movement compares today's live numbers with the end of the last gameweek, and the chart is drawn from saved days only (agreed 2026-10-19; the label is "Since GW6", the baseline's gameweek). Why: the number next to a bar has to add up with the bar (strength now minus the baseline), and the bars are live; a comparison made only from saved files could differ from the bar by a day of ratings moving. "Since the end of last gameweek" and not "7 days ago" so a change means "this gameweek", and the label stays true mid-gameweek. The split into "same players" and "different players" is exact (strength is an average of the eleven), where a by-piece split of the stayers would have needed each player's pieces saved (about five times the file). Cost: the chart's last point is the latest saved day (up to a day old) so it can differ a little from the bar next to it; and see the Known issues entry
- Deals are judged on the points players actually scored since the deal, and the wording comes from templates (agreed 2026-10-18). Why: the first idea was a private "report card" of the app's own suggestions I didn't act on. That needed the app to record its suggestions every day and a week of waiting, and "who missed out" wasn't what I wanted anyway: I wanted the league-facing version, a compliment or a roast for real trades. The Draft site already serves real trades, claims and finished gameweeks' points, so real deals can be judged today with nothing recorded. Points rather than ratings because points are what happened. Templates rather than AI for the reasons the banter uses them (instant, free, can't get a number wrong); the same facts could be handed to the recap's AI later for fresher jokes. Cost: raw points ignore whether the player started and favour forwards over defenders (see Known issues)
- A trade only gets a winner when its swing beats luck (agreed 2026-10-18). Why: on the two real trades the swing was 4 points after two gameweeks, and one lucky goal moves a single player by 5 to 8 points a week, so a fixed bar would crown winners and roast losers on noise. The bar grows with the number of players and gameweeks (deal_size), so a big trade needs a big gap and the same rule works for the weekly line and the long-run verdict. The verdict wording stays "so far" until DEAL_SETTLED_WEEKS (4) gameweeks have finished. Cost: a one-for-one swap needs about a 7-point gap in its first week, so "too close to call" is the common answer early on
- Pickup of the week and Dropped and regretted are plain superlatives with a 6-point minimum, not a luck test (2026-10-18). Why: trades are rare (2 in the first 6 gameweeks) and there are about 10 claims a week, so these keep the card alive; they say what happened ("scored 9, the player dropped for him scored 1") and don't claim the manager was wise or foolish. Cost: on 2026-10-18 none of GW6's 10 claims reached 6, so the card had no pickup that week
- "Nobody bid" means nobody in the last 7 days, not "since the deadline" (2026-10-18). Why: claims may not be possible while a gameweek is being played, so "no bid since the deadline" could be true of every player for free. Cost: a week is a rough stand-in for "this week"
- "Your team this week" shows the best lineup and the best one in a different formation, side by side as pitches (agreed 2026-10-16, "a visual, side by side"). Why: the user wanted to see what the team should look like and that there is another good option using the subs. "Second best" means another formation, because that is what brings subs in and gives a different shape to compare; the alternative (same formation, one swap) shows a smaller, less useful difference. Players with no game that week are benched (the point of "for the week"), so the lineup's strength can differ from League squads' number in a blank week. No captain pick, because it wasn't asked for; the highest-rated starter with a game would be a small add. Cost: see the Known issues entry
- Players by club lives on the main page under Waiver targets, and free agents carry a claim (agreed 2026-10-16). Why: I wanted the club list "for the regular waiver targets", with available players and owned ones in the same list, the owned ones showing the +/- of a trade. The trade page already had this for owned players, so this reuses its data (one new field, "claim") rather than a second system, and the waiver cards stay as they were (the top five picks, with reasons). The claim drops my weakest player in his position, because that always leaves the strongest squad and is what a waiver swap would drop; a lift under 0.5 shows "Wouldn't improve your best eleven" rather than a tiny number. The page shows every player of a club (agreed), and Check this trade now carries the trade in its address so the trade page opens with it ticked and run. Cost: see the two Players by club entries under Known issues

Why: I asked for "a trade for each Premier League team" and to click a team and see all of it; every trade has to be agreed with one manager, so each player carries who owns him. Ranked by my biggest gain, not "fair first": on the real league (2026-10-13) almost every one-for-one trade is zero-sum ("me +3.4, them −3.5") and the fair ones are all tiny (+0.5, which is the minimum), so fair-first put a +0.5 trade above a +3.7 one. The tag and both numbers carry the fairness instead. A first plan had a top 3 per club and an "Only fair trades" switch; both went when the whole squad was shown. - The name is asked once, in a pop-up, and there is no share bar (asked for 2026-10-13). Why: friends who open the plain link are asked who they are, so there are no special links to hand out, and the card shows only the visitor's own matches instead of a picker that invites looking at everyone's. Cost: it's a convenience, not a lock: Not you? lets anyone choose a different name, because the data is public and the server has no way to tell who is asking (see the next decision for what a real lock would take)
- "Who are you?" is a list of names with no code (agreed 2026-10-13). Why: the card only shows things anyone in the league can already see, so a code would protect nothing; a zip code is personal data the app has nowhere safe to keep (no database, and the GitHub repo is public, checked 2026-10-13), is easy to guess, is shared by neighbours and doesn't exist the same way in every country. Cost: anyone can pick someone else's name and see that person's card (public data). If a real "this is me" is ever needed: a personal link per friend with a token worked out from the team ID and one secret kept in Render's environment (stateless, nothing collected), which the page would store and then remove from the address
- Strength history is recorded daily and from now on, with no back-fill (agreed 2026-10-12). Why: the feeds only hold today's form, points per game and fixtures, so last week's rating can't be rebuilt, and every day not written down is lost for good. Quiet days are skipped so the daily job doesn't commit (and Render doesn't redeploy) when nothing moved. The save is a separate step that can fail without stopping the results snapshot. Cost: nothing on the site shows movement yet, and there is no history before 2026-10-12
- "Running hot / cold" is a note on the player, not a change to the rating: it compares form with points per game, which is the same stat over a shorter window, and only says whether recent games differ from the season. It needs MIN_MINUTES so two games can't make a trend. Found by looking at the real league on 2026-10-12: the league's top team (Khanh's) had a form piece well above everyone's, but its starters' form averaged 5.3 against 5.4 points per game, so it was a consistently good squad and not a spike; only McGinn and Jacquet (hot) and White (cold) were away from their average. So a big form piece does not by itself mean a streak
- The recap's AI writes words only. Every score, margin, outlook word, star and table move is worked out in Python (recap_facts, tested) and the prompt forbids new numbers, so the AI can't get a score wrong, only phrase things oddly. The scoreboard under the text shows the same numbers.
- Recaps are written when someone opens the page and then kept, not by a scheduled job. A scheduled GitHub job would need the API key as a GitHub secret, would run on GitHub's servers (which Cloudflare may block, as the Known issues warn for snapshots) and would miss match days unless it ran every hour. Cost: the first visitor after a stage change waits a few seconds, and the kept recaps live in memory, so a restart or redeploy (the daily snapshot commit redeploys) writes the current stage again, about one extra call.
- RECAP_MODEL is an environment variable with no default in the code: the model can be changed in Render without touching the code, and no model name sits in the repo. Until it's set the plain recap shows.
- The final recap tells the story and doesn't list results: the page already has a Results list, so repeating every score in the written text showed the same thing twice. The text is the talking points (biggest win, closest match, highest and lowest score), the table moves are bullets, and there are no stars or next-up line (asked for after the first version went live). Everything that isn't the written text comes from Python, so it's the same with or without the AI.
- Stages are match days, not single games, to keep it to about 3-5 recaps (and AI calls) a gameweek. The first games of a gameweek get one early look before the first whole day is done.
- The league hub never puts a team ID in its link, and never reads one from it. Your highlight comes from this browser's memory of the team you last used on the main page. Why: the link is the thing people copy from the address bar and send to friends, and a team ID in it made your team look like "you" for them. Cost: if a browser blocks storage there's no highlight, and the main page's link to the hub no longer carries the team either (it relies on the same memory).
- Snapshots live in the repo's history/ folder on main, not a database or a separate branch. The repo is a free permanent store with nothing new to set up, and the app just reads local files. Cost: each daily change is a commit to main, so Render redeploys that day (harmless; the cache just resets). If that gets annoying, move history to its own branch and read it over HTTP, or use a database.
- "Worth doing" is judged on my best-eleven lift, not on the rating gain, and capped at 2 (agreed 2026-10-09). Why: the rating gain says how much better the claim is than the drop, but a claim who doesn't start for me wins no matches (on the real league, 2026-10-09, one manager's Penders for Benitez had a rating gain of 35 and changed his best eleven by 0.0, while George for Gomez had a gain of 23 and lifted it by 2.1), and with 5 swaps on the page everything looked equally urgent. The cap keeps "worth doing" meaning "do these first". It's waivers only because trades are picked by me, so "barely changes" already says it; a "no trade needed" message would need the trade finder
- The "worth holding" note is a note, not a rule: the swap is still suggested and nothing is hidden or auto-kept. Why: whether to wait for an injured star is a judgement about your bench and the claim on offer, and the Keep button already does the protecting. It only says what the numbers show (a proven season, out or slumping now), never that a player "usually scores more late in the season", because the feeds can't show that (see Known issues)
- How long a proven player is out decides the advice, with my numbers (agreed 2026-10-09): out about a month or less (4 gameweeks) is worth holding, out 10 or more is fine to let go, and in between is your call. 10 is where the "until the break" view stops looking, and a middle band means no cliff between 4 and 5. A long absence still shows a note (with Keep offered) instead of vanishing, so you can see why the app isn't telling you to hold. The note also says how many gameweeks are left after he's back, because the same absence matters more late in the season. Absolute gameweek counts, not a share of the season left
- A doubt only counts as "out" at 50% or less, or if he also missed the latest gameweek. Found by running the notes over all 180 players the league owns on 2026-10-08: with "any doubt counts", Rice, Palmer, Isak and Semenyo (75%, playing 90 every week) were flagged
- Past seasons come from the classic site by player `code`, and only for the few players being dropped, so a page load costs at most 5 extra requests. The bars (HOLD_POINTS) are fixed numbers, not a ranking of everyone, because ranking would need a history request for all ~700 players; on 2026-10-08 I checked them against last season's points of the league's owned players who played 900+ minutes (132 of 180): about 3 in 4 of them fall under each bar

One worker (so there's one shared 10-minute cache and it fits the free plan's memory) with 4 threads (so a few people can load at once). Timeout 60s because a league load makes several FPL requests.

DNA carries the flags as small dots so they aren't lost when the grid is hidden

The page says so, and the wire figure is a pointer to clubs worth a look, not a forecast

Trades only move a player between two managers, so they say nothing about the wire, and the draft that opened the season would drown everything else.
